# Methods

How every result in this repository is produced, phase by phase. Parameter
values live in [`config.yaml`](../config.yaml); this file explains them. Checks
of the method and their results are in [validation.md](validation.md); the
history of the choices (what changed when, and why) is in
[decisions.md](decisions.md).

Conventions: everything is projected to EPSG:32633 (UTM 33N, metres). Numbers
quoted in the documentation come from files in `output/`; tables are
generated by `scripts/build_readme_tables.py`.

## 2. Phase 0: walk to transit

Entry point `scripts/run_accessibility.py`; code in `src/berlin_access/`
(`pipeline.py`, `network.py`, `gtfs_modes.py`, `population.py`).

### 2.1 Reporting units
One geometry source for all levels: the 542 LOR 2021 Planungsräume. Each
carries its Bezirksregion (143) and district (12); a building is assigned to a
Planungsraum by location, and its Bezirksregion and district follow from the
code, so the levels nest exactly. The city outline is the union of the
Planungsräume. Planungsräume are the closest official unit to a Kiez.

### 2.2 Walk network
`pyrosm` walking network (`network_type="walking"`, one row per segment
between consecutive OSM nodes), projected length in metres.
- **Undirected**: every segment is walkable both ways.
- Parallel segments between the same two nodes keep the shortest length.
- Only the largest connected component is kept (nodes outside it counted in
  `run_metadata.json`).
- OSM data is read for the city plus `network_buffer_m` (3 km), so a walk to a
  Berlin stop may use a path just outside the city.

### 2.3 Buildings and residents
- Every OSM building polygon becomes one representative point (inside the
  polygon), assigned to its Planungsraum and snapped to the nearest network
  node. Buildings more than `max_building_snap_m` (250 m) from the network
  are excluded and counted.
- **Residents (dasymetric allocation).** Residents are known per Planungsraum
  only (register, table T2). They are split among the Planungsraum's
  buildings in proportion to estimated residential floor area:
  - a building is a residential candidate unless its OSM `building` tag is
    clearly non-residential (garage, shed, allotment house, office, school,
    industrial, ...; list in `population.NON_RESIDENTIAL`); for untyped
    `building=yes`, `building:use` decides where mapped; footprint at least
    `min_residential_footprint_m2` (40 m²);
  - weight = footprint area x storeys (`building:levels`); missing storeys
    filled with the median of known values in the Planungsraum, then the
    city-wide median;
  - residents of a Planungsraum without any candidate building cannot be
    placed and are listed in `output/population_allocation_check.csv`.
- **Older residents.** Each building gets its Planungsraum's share of
  residents aged 65+ (location within a Planungsraum is unknown). Reported as
  `weighting = residents_65plus`, at 1.0 m/s.

### 2.4 Stops and modes
A stop has a mode if at least one trip of that mode serves it. Mode comes from
`route_type`:

| route_type | mode |
|---|---|
| 109 | S-Bahn |
| 1, 400, 401 | U-Bahn |
| 0, 900 | Tram |
| 2, 100, 106 | Regionalbahn |
| 3, 700-799 | Bus |
| 1000 | excluded (ferry) |

Names are used only for route types missing from this table, and only for
`S<digit>`, `U<digit>`, `RE<digit>`, `RB<digit>` (`M` lines are both MetroTram
and MetroBus). A 700-series route is always a bus, so rail-replacement buses
named U2, U5 etc. stay buses. A check per route type is written to
`output/gtfs_route_type_check.csv` on every run.

**Combined modes** (`combined_modes` in `config.yaml`): "S- or U-Bahn" and
"Any mode" are the walk to whichever member mode is nearer, computed per
building as the minimum of the member distances.

### 2.5 City limit and frequency
- **Main run: only stops inside Berlin** (`stop_buffer_m: 0`): the study is
  about the city as lived and governed. Sensitivity run: stops up to 1 km
  outside Berlin also count (`output/*_stopbuffer_1000m.csv`).
- **Frequent stops** (`frequency` in `config.yaml`): a stop of a mode counts
  if it has at least one departure of that mode every 10 min on average,
  07:00-09:00 on Tuesday 10 November 2026 (a school-term weekday), counted
  per GTFS stop point from the timetable (calendar plus calendar_dates).
  Results in `output/*_frequent_10min.csv`, counts in
  `output/stops_frequency_by_mode.csv`.
- Stops more than `max_stop_snap_m` (250 m) from a network node are dropped.

### 2.6 Distances and speeds
One multi-source Dijkstra per mode (`scipy.sparse.csgraph.dijkstra`) from a
virtual source linked to every stop node, link weight = the stop's snap
distance; each building's snap distance is added. Time = distance / speed:
main run 1.3 m/s, sensitivity 1.0 and 1.4 m/s.

### 2.7 Aggregation
For each level (district, Bezirksregion, Planungsraum), mode and speed:
median, quartiles, mean and share beyond 15 and 30 min, weighted by residents
(main), by residents 65+, and by buildings (for comparison). No building is
dropped for being far away. Units with fewer than `min_unit_residents` (100)
residents are flagged `low_population`. Comparing levels: results depend on
unit size and shape (modifiable areal unit problem); compare units within one
level.

### 2.8 Outputs
`output/walk_time_by_{district,bezirksregion,planungsraum}[_stopbuffer_1000m|_frequent_10min].csv`,
`output/median_walk_min_<speed>mps.csv`, `output/population_allocation_check.csv`,
`output/stops_by_mode.csv`, `output/gtfs_route_type_check.csv`,
`output/run_metadata.json`; per-building distances in
`data/derived/building_walk_dist.parquet` (git-ignored). The parsed network
and buildings are cached in `data/derived/`, keyed by input hashes.

## 3. Phase 1: angular segment analysis

Entry point `scripts/run_segments.py`; code in `src/berlin_access/segments.py`.
cityseer 5.8.0 (pinned; the cleaning recipe used is a private function).

### 3.1 Segment map
1. **Ways**: all public OSM ways (pyrosm `all_public`), then removed:
   separately mapped sidewalks (`footway=sidewalk`, they duplicate the street
   centre line), motorways, forestry and field tracks (`highway=track`), the
   non-walkable types cityseer's default query excludes, ways with
   `foot=no/private`, areas, indoor ways, levels -2 to -5. Not pyrosm's walking
   network: it omits streets whose sidewalks are mapped separately.
2. **Primal graph** with the attributes cityseer's OSM loader writes (names,
   ref, highway class, tunnel/bridge).
3. **Cleaning**: cityseer's own OSM recipe (`io._auto_clean_network`),
   unchanged, with park, plaza and parking areas taken from the local PBF
   instead of the Overpass API. It deduplicates overlapping edges, removes
   footways in parks and forests, merges parallel carriageways by road class
   (trunk 40 m to tertiary 24 m), consolidates complex intersections,
   links gapped endings, removes dangling slivers, and cleans minor streets at
   4 m and 8 m. The result approximates a road-centre-line segment map.
   The result depends on the order in which nodes are visited, which follows
   Python's per-process hash seed; all scripts therefore run with
   `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` (validation §8).
4. **Dual graph** (`graphs.nx_to_dual`): segments become nodes; the turn angle
   between adjacent segments is the angular cost.

### 3.2 Study area and edge effects
The whole city is analysed as **one network** (Berlin + 2 km buffer, the
largest radius). Segments are `live` if an end node lies inside Berlin;
non-live segments carry paths but get no results. Each live segment is
assigned to the district containing its midpoint.

### 3.3 Residential streets
All streets stay in the network for routing, but statistics, rankings and
maps cover only **residential streets**: segments with a building with
allocated residents within `segment_residential_buffer_m` (50 m). Removing
non-residential streets from the graph instead would cut routes through parks
or industrial areas and change the residential streets' own values.

### 3.4 Measures (radii 400, 800, 1200, 2000 m, metric radius along the path)
- **Angular (simplest path)**: cost `c` = angular change in degrees; angular
  depth = `c / 90` (a 90° turn = 1, the Depthmap / Hillier convention).
  Integration: harmonic closeness `1 / (1 + c/90)`, farness, total angular
  depth TD. Choice: angular betweenness, each unordered pair of segments
  counted once, origin and destination excluded.
- **Length-weighted angular choice** (`cc_betweenness_<r>_ang_lw`, each route
  counted by origin length x destination length), supplementary.
- **Metric (shortest path)** closeness and betweenness, for contrast.
- **NAIN and NACH** (Hillier, Yang and Turner 2012): NAIN = NC^1.2 / (TD + 2),
  NACH = log(CH + 1) / log(TD + 3); NC = node count including the origin,
  CH = angular choice. Differences from Depthmap: choice counts each pair
  once; the segment map comes from automatic cleaning, not a drawn map.

### 3.5 Outputs
`output/segments_berlin.gpkg` (all segments and measures, git-ignored, over
100 MB), `output/segments_berlin_{summary,by_district,top10,main_streets}.csv`,
`output/segments_berlin_metadata.json`, NACH maps; the
Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg pilot (district + 2 km) as
`output/segments_friedrichshain-kreuzberg*`.

## 4. Phase 2: Place Syntax Tool inputs and attraction reach

Entry point `scripts/run_pst_inputs.py`; code in `src/berlin_access/pst.py`;
step-by-step guide for QGIS: [pst_howto.md](pst_howto.md). PST dialogs and
options were read from the PST 3.3.2 source (github.com/SMoG-Chalmers/PST).

- **Area**: Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg pilot, segments within district + 2 km,
  origins inside the district.
- **Network**: the citywide Phase 1 segment map, cropped and **split into
  straight two-point lines** (PST accepts no other line type; pieces share end
  points, so distances are unchanged). Lines connect only at shared end
  points; where two lines meet without both having an end point there
  (bridges, tunnels), a point is written to the `unlinks` layer.
- **Origins**: residential buildings (allocated residents > 0), with residents
  and residents 65+.
- **Destinations: stations, not stop points**: VBB stop points are single
  platforms or poles, so they are grouped by IFOPT stop place (first three
  parts of the stop id). Station point = mean of its served stop points.
  0/1 columns per mode (`sb`, `ub`, `tr`, `bu`, `rb`), `su` (S- or U-Bahn),
  frequent flags (`<mode>f`, `anyf`). Stations inside Berlin only.
- **Python cross-check** (walking distance): each point joined to its closest
  line, connection length counted, walking along the lines; attraction reach =
  stations with the column = 1 within 400 / 800 m; attraction distance = walk
  to the nearest such station, capped at 2 km.
- `scripts/compare_pst_results.py` compares PST output (run by hand in QGIS)
  with the cross-check, matching buildings on `osm_id`.

Outputs: `output/pst/pst_inputs_<district>.gpkg` (QGIS layers),
`output/pst/python_reach_<district>*.csv|json`,
`output/pst/pst_results_<district>.gpkg` (PST output),
`output/pst/pst_vs_python_<district>.csv`.

## 5. Phase 3: metric access versus configuration

Entry point `scripts/run_phase3.py`.

- **Per residential building**: walk time at 1.3 m/s to the nearest S- or
  U-Bahn station and to the nearest frequent stop of any mode (Phase 0), and
  the measures of its **nearest live street segment** (Phase 1): NAIN and
  NACH at 800 and 2000 m, metric closeness at 800 m.
- **Correlations**: Spearman rank correlation per district and for Berlin, at
  two levels: buildings (unweighted) and Planungsräume (resident-weighted
  medians, units with at least 100 residents). Both levels are reported
  because aggregation itself changes correlations.
- **Divergence**: Planungsräume are split into citywide thirds of the
  resident-weighted median walk to S- or U-Bahn and of the median NAIN at
  2000 m. *Close to transit, segregated* = shortest-walk third and
  lowest-NAIN third; *far from transit, integrated* = longest-walk third and
  highest-NAIN third; *concordant* = close and integrated, or far and
  segregated; everything else *middle*. NAIN at 2000 m matches the scale at
  which people choose a station (a 10 to 25 min walk).
- **Pilot**: Phase 2 attraction reach (stations within 800 m, frequent stops
  within 400 m) against the same measures.

Outputs: `output/phase3/correlations.csv`,
`output/phase3/divergence_by_planungsraum.csv`,
`output/phase3/reach_vs_centrality_friedrichshain-kreuzberg.csv`,
`output/phase3/summary.json`, `output/maps/phase3_divergence.png`.

## 6. Maps

`scripts/make_maps.py` (Phase 0), `scripts/run_segments.py` (Phase 1),
`scripts/run_phase3.py` (Phase 3).
- Phase 0 maps share one classed scale for all modes (5 or less, 5-10, 10-15,
  15-30, over 30 min) in five steps of one blue hue, checked with a palette
  validator (monotone lightness, minimum step gap, lightest step at least 2:1
  against the background). Units under 100 residents are hatched.
- Phase 1 maps show NACH in quintile classes (NACH is relative), residential
  streets coloured, other streets gray.
- The Phase 3 map uses two categorical hues for the two divergent classes and
  grays for the rest.
- Where a mode does not run (trams in the west), the darkest class means "no
  service nearby", not a measured everyday walk.

## 7. Limitations

- Residents are placed within a Planungsraum by an OSM floor-area estimate;
  storeys are mapped for only part of the buildings, and mixed-use buildings
  count as residential. Totals per Planungsraum are exact.
- GTFS platform coordinates are used, not station entrances; deep U-Bahn
  stations can be far from their street entrances.
- Nearest stop only: frequency enters only as a yes/no filter, counted per
  stop point over all lines of a mode.
- Older residents are spread within a Planungsraum like all residents.
- Network quality depends on OSM mapping; the Phase 1 segment map comes from
  automatic cleaning.
- Angular measures describe the street network's configuration, not
  footfall: busy shopping streets that are not through-routes (e.g.
  pedestrian zones) can rank low.

Open questions are tracked as GitHub issues.
